Chapter III

The long way round

Measure twice. Cut once. Then measure the thing you cut, because it has already changed. — workshop proverb, source unverified

There is a version of this chapter that takes the short way — states the method, shows the result, moves on. This is not that version. The long way round exists because the short way lied about how the work actually happened, smoothing over the three false starts that mattered more than the fourth, successful attempt.

A note, book-style: this is the same margin-note mechanism from the single-post template — footnotes in a book are the most natural home for it, arguably more than a blog post.

The first false start

So: the first attempt. It is tempting to begin a chapter about process with the clean version, but the clean version is a lie of omission — it erases exactly the part a reader setting out to do similar work actually needs. What follows instead is closer to a field notebook than a manual, written in the order it happened rather than the order that would make the most sense in retrospect.

The image to the left is from that first pass — kept deliberately, not cropped out, because the failure is part of the record this chapter is trying to keep straight. Text continues to wrap around it at this width, the way a printed book would set a marginal illustration beside running text rather than breaking the page for it.

By the second attempt the shape of the problem had changed enough that the original photographs stopped being useful as documentation and started being useful as comparison instead — which is its own small lesson about when to stop referring back to early material and when to let it recede.

A wider view

After the wide view, the text resumes at the normal column width. The point of the break above wasn't informational so much as rhythmic — a place to let the reader's eye rest before the next stretch of close reading.

"The third attempt is the one nobody photographs, because by then you've stopped believing it's going anywhere" — a note to self, scrawled in the margin of an actual paper notebook, long before any of this had a name.

And yet it was the third attempt that actually taught the thing the fourth attempt merely executed. This is the part that gets cut from the short version every time, and it's the part this book is specifically trying not to cut.

"The failure isn't the part you cut for time. It's the part the whole chapter was actually about."
