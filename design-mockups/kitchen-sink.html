Image, caption, credit

Caption sits directly under the image in regular weight; the credit line drops to a smaller, muted italic directly beneath it — matching the project's standing rule that captions live underneath, not overlaid.

What this page is testing: H1–H3, paragraphs, blockquote, inline/block code, lists, hr, a full-bleed image with caption+credit, and margin notes — all at once, so gaps show up here instead of on a real post.

Margin notes, honestly: this is a pure-CSS, no-JavaScript technique (the classic "Tufte CSS" pattern) — a hidden checkbox + label pair gives mobile a tap-to-reveal note inline, while wide screens float the note into the right-hand gutter via a negative margin. It is not pixel-perfect: when two references land close together, or a paragraph runs long, the floats just stack in document order rather than snapping exactly level with their reference (see the two notes above collide slightly). A JS-assisted version could measure each reference's exact position and nudge its note to match — more precise, but a real dependency. Worth deciding whether "roughly level" is good enough before we build further on this.

Authoring, looking ahead: Obsidian already has native footnote syntax ([^1] / [^1]: text), and Quartz renders those as ordinary bottom-of-page footnotes by default. Turning your normal footnotes into margin notes instead — rather than inventing new syntax to type — means swapping in a custom Quartz transform for footnotes. That's a real build step, not a mockup concern, but it's the automation-first path: write footnotes the normal Obsidian way, let the build turn them into this.
