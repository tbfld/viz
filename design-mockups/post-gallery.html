Introduction

Some posts have one image, maybe two — the automatic stacked treatment handles those fine without any thought. This one has twelve, shot within about forty minutes, and stacking twelve full-width images one after another would turn a quick look into a scroll marathon. This is the dedicated gallery view instead: a grid, not a scroll, with each image still full-size and captioned on request rather than cropped down to a uniform thumbnail.

About Colophon

What this page is testing: a dedicated per-post gallery for a run of images too long for the automatic "stacked" blog treatment, with a lightbox for viewing them full-size — the gap identified when the book template's four image layouts were built (those are for one or two images at a time within running text; this is for many, with no body text in between).

Layout: CSS multi-column masonry, not a uniform grid — each image keeps its own natural aspect ratio (no cropping), and break-inside: avoid keeps an image with its caption. No JS layout math at all; the browser handles the column-balancing. Column count steps from 3 → 2 → 1 at its own breakpoints, independent of the sidebar's 720px collapse.

Lightbox: click any image to open it full-size with its caption; arrow buttons/keys and Escape all work. It's vanilla JS, no library, and the scrim stays dark regardless of light/dark mode — maximizing photo contrast is the point of a lightbox, the same convention most photo viewers use.

Still open: this is the per-post case only, per your answer — a site-wide gallery page aggregating images across posts is a related but separate question, worth coming back to once this grid component is confirmed.
