Technoculture seems to have a love affair with this kind of ‘proxemics’ theory, first articulated (AFAIK) by the cultural anthropologist Edward T. Hall in the early ’60s — I’d argue as part of a quickly growing attention to the spatial aspects of sociability and lifeworlds (for ex, Edmund “Ted” Carpenter’s 1959 quasi-monograph, Eskimo, which experimented with book design to convey unfamiliar spatial constructs. That awareness a basic tool for anthropologists, but it wasn’t until the early ’90s (read: the internet) that proxemics took off, often under names like “zones of engagement” — and, strangely, mostly by people working <cough> “at the intersection of” quant marketing, business school, and social psychology.
