Adding to that last post about the Farquhar globe[^1]: transparent 'plastics' had been around for decades — since Otto Röhm invented plexiglass[^2] in 1933 at least — but it was in the late '50s / early '60s that a new confluence — of spaceflight, OT1H, and OT2H new media (in particular, the explosion of magazines[^3]) that created new venues for PR images like this. Three certifiable rocket scientists, surrounded by avatars of the information age in the form of an IBM 7090 mainframe,[^4] convening around something like a Farquhar celestial globe,[^5] like architects around a model of a world where, as Marx and Engels put it, all that was solid had melted into air. My retort: a world where, conversely, all that was aerial was condensing into new kinds of solids. 
 
It's worth noting that all three — von Braun, Hölzer, and Rees — were Operation Paperclip alumni, though tbh I'm not a fan of how OP's is fast becoming a sort of MK ULTRA twin in conspiracy-fanboi culture. At a time when "alt history" and "gaming out" future scenarios are all the rage, it’s not an accident that people are forgetting how to game out the *past* — in this case by asking whether the US or USSR should’ve snapped up all the scientists, engineers, etc. Not all of them were totally stoked about fascism.[^6]
