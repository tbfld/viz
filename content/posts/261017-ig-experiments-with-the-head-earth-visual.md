Experiments with the head / earth visual homology had two aspects: 
 
OT1H, it suggests a growing intuition that the inherited ~poetic assumption that man is but a speck and the world infinite was crumbling. In its place came a new recognition — fueled by globe-spanning colonial empires, the territorial expansion of the "Great War" (WW1) into "World War" (WW2, obv), and later the rise of aviation and space flight — that humanity was "outgrowing" the earth.

OT2H, a less mythical recognition that the homology presented a 'teachable moment.' Our native sense of human bodies, something we all share — a bit like Anatole France's dictum that "the law, in its majestic equality, forbids rich and poor alike to sleep under bridges" ― offered an equitable way to show how conventional maps conveyed a distorted image of the world. So, for ex, "\[Otto] Neurath asked cartographer Karl Peucker to make a sketch showing the distortion of area in the Mercator projection. This was then adapted at the \[Isotype] museum into a diagram with a human figure overlaid in order to convey the degree of distortion more effectively" (from Christopher Burke, "The Atlas *Gesellschaft und Wirtschaft* \[Society and Economy]," in Burke, Erik Kindel, and Sue Walker, *Isotope: Design and Contexts, 1925–1971* (London: Hyphen, 2013), p. 192.
