Promo image for a 14” (35.56cm) transparent globe manufactured by Farquhar Transparent Globes, a Philadelphia-based concern that made (you guess it) transparent globes for education and government uses. (The founder, Robert Farquhar, seems not to have been related to Robert W. Farquhar, the NASA mission designer.[1]) Experiments along these lines (and many others) began in earnest during WW2 but really took off (so to speak) with the rise of spaceflight. Individually, they seem curious, but when you start to assemble them more systematically you begin to see a profound reconceptualization of the earth — and, I would argue, a *mediation* of it. (Unfortunately, I’ve lost my source for this digitization of this image.)
